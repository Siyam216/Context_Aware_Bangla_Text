\begin{abstract}
\noindent Automated understanding of low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Bengali is severely hindered in informal social media discourse by colloquial idioms, orthographic variability, and semantic inversion caused by sarcasm. Traditional natural language processing (NLP) pipelines that analyze sentiment, sarcasm, and hate speech in isolation fail to resolve sentences where surface-level positive sentiment masks caustic sarcasm or covert hostility. In this work, we propose an end-to-end context-aware multi-task classification framework spanning three distinct linguistic tasks across 218,371 annotated Bengali sentences (156,010 for sentiment polarity, 12,089 for sarcasm detection, and 50,272 for hate speech identification). We systematically formulate, train, and benchmark three progressive modeling paradigms representing key milestones in modern NLP: (1) a statistical machine learning baseline using sublinear TF-IDF with class-balanced Logistic Regression; (2) a deep sequential recurrent architecture utilizing 128-dimensional dense word representations coupled with a 2-layer stacked Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network; and (3) a state-of-the-art transformer architecture fine-tuning the 110-million parameter pre-trained \texttt{BanglaBERT} model on GPU hardware. Furthermore, we develop a custom negation-preserving preprocessing pipeline that explicitly protects Bengali negation markers from aggressive stopword stripping. Rigorous empirical evaluation demonstrates that fine-tuned \texttt{BanglaBERT} establishes new task champions, securing \textbf{91.59\% accuracy and 91.58\% Macro F1} on hate speech detection, and \textbf{76.76\% accuracy and 74.89\% Macro F1} on sarcasm detection. Finally, we design a dynamic context-aware decision engine that resolves semantic inversion and deploy the complete pipeline in a production-ready interactive Streamlit dashboard for real-time text analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
Bengali is the seventh most spoken language globally, with over 300 million native speakers primarily distributed across Bangladesh and India. Despite its massive demographic footprint, computational linguistics for Bengali remains constrained by low-resource challenges, high morphological inflection, extensive diglossia (stark differences between formal \textit{Sadhu/Cholit} and colloquial speech), and a severe lack of standardized syntactic resources~\cite{alam2021banglabert}. 

In modern digital spaces, user-generated Bengali comments are characterized by complex rhetorical devices, informal phonetic transcriptions, grammatical omissions, and implicit hostility. Among these challenges, the phenomenon of \textbf{Sarcastic Inversion} poses a formidable barrier to automated sentiment analysis. Consider the canonical Bengali online review:
\begin{center}
\textit{``Bah! Ki oshadharon service, tin ghonta opekkha koreo kaj holo na!''} \\
\small{(Translation: ``Wow! What extraordinary service, even after waiting three hours nothing was done!'')}
\end{center}

Under standard bag-of-words or naive lexicon matching, salient positive lexical triggers such as \textit{``oshadharon''} (extraordinary) overwhelmingly bias the model toward predicting a \textbf{Positive} sentiment. However, the subsequent contextual clause \textit{``kaj holo na''} (nothing was done) inverts the entire semantic polarity into a strongly \textbf{Negative} sentiment. A standalone sentiment analyzer lacks the cognitive capability to resolve this tension unless it is co-informed by a dedicated \textbf{Sarcasm Detection} component. Concurrently, online platforms must screen for malicious intent, cyberbullying, and hate speech without misclassifying benign satirical humor as abusive toxicity.

\section{Related Work \& Curriculum Mapping}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evolution of Bengali NLP}
Early work in Bengali natural language processing predominantly relied on shallow morphological stemmers and rule-based sentiment lexicons~\cite{das2010sentiwordnet}. With the advent of statistical learning, n-gram term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) paired with Support Vector Machines (SVM) or Naive Bayes became standard baselines. However, n-gram representations suffer from sparsity and fail to model continuous semantics or non-local dependencies.

The introduction of distributed word representations (Word2Vec, FastText) enabled deep recurrent neural networks, specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), to capture sequential patterns~\cite{hochreiter1997long}. In recent years, Transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert} and ELECTRA~\cite{clark2020electra} have revolutionized contextual representations. For Bengali, models such as \texttt{BanglaBERT}~\cite{alam2021banglabert} pre-trained on tens of gigabytes of raw crawled text have established state-of-the-art benchmarks on sentiment analysis and named entity recognition.

\subsection{Alignment with CSE 4122 Lab Syllabus}
This project is systematically grounded in the pedagogical milestones of the CSE 4122 (Natural Language Processing Sessional) curriculum, bridging foundational theory with production deployment:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Lab Session 1 (Text Normalization \& Regex):} Implemented custom regex filtering, Unicode zero-width stripper, and the Negation Preservation Pipeline.
    \item \textbf{Lab Session 2 (N-Gram Language Models \& TF-IDF):} Extracted unigram and bigram sublinear TF-IDF feature matrices across 20,000 vocabularies.
    \item \textbf{Lab Session 3 (Continuous Embeddings \& Classification):} Trained 128-dimensional dense continuous vector representations and implemented cost-sensitive Logistic Regression.
    \item \textbf{Lab Session 4 (Deep Recurrent Sequence Modeling):} Constructed PyTorch-based 2-layer stacked Bidirectional LSTM networks with spatial dropout.
    \item \textbf{Lab Session 5 (Pre-trained Transformers):} Fine-tuned the 110M parameter \texttt{BanglaBERT} model utilizing HuggingFace Transformers on GPU hardware.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Negation-Preserving Preprocessing}
A primary source of degradation in Bengali text classification is naive stopword removal. In standard NLP pipelines, high-frequency functional tokens are discarded. However, in Bengali, negation tokens---such as \textit{``na''} (no/not), \textit{``noy''} (is not), \textit{``nei''} (is absent), \textit{``kokhono na''} (never), and \textit{``chhara''} (without)---are grammatical stop words that dictate the entire polarity of a clause. Stripping them inverts negative statements into positive ones, completely invalidating sarcasm and sentiment classification.

We developed Algorithm~\ref{alg:negation_clean} to purify text while enforcing strict preservation of negation operators.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Negation-Preserving Text Normalization}
\label{alg:negation_clean}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Raw Bengali String $S_{raw}$, Negation Set $\mathcal{N}$
\Ensure Normalized Bengali String $S_{clean}$
\State $S \gets \text{RemoveURLsAndHTML}(S_{raw})$
\State $S \gets \text{StripZeroWidthAndEmojis}(S)$ \Comment{\small U+200B--U+200D}
\State $S \gets \text{NormalizeBengaliCharacters}(S)$
\State $Tokens \gets \text{TokenizeOnWhitespace}(S)$
\State $FilteredTokens \gets [\,]$
\For{$t \in Tokens$}
    \If{$t \in \mathcal{N}$}
        \State $FilteredTokens.\text{append}(t)$ \Comment{\small Protected Negation}
    \ElsIf{$t \in \mathcal{SW}_{generic}$}
        \State \textbf{continue} \Comment{\small Discard generic stopword}
    \Else
        \State $FilteredTokens.\text{append}(t)$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\text{Join}(FilteredTokens, \text{`` ''})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Methodological Framework}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
We implemented three progressive modeling paradigms representing distinct theoretical approaches to natural language understanding.

\subsection{Paradigm 1: Sublinear TF-IDF + Logistic Regression}
As our statistical baseline, sentences are mapped to sparse vector spaces using word n-grams up to order 2 ($1 \le n \le 2$). To mitigate the distorting effect of excessively repetitive terms in long social media posts, we apply sublinear scaling to the term frequency ($tf$):
\begin{equation}
w_{t,d} = (1 + \log(tf_{t,d})) \times \log\left( \frac{1 + N}{1 + df_t} \right) + 1
\end{equation}
A vocabulary capacity of 20,000 features was extracted. We trained multinomial Logistic Regression with inverse class weighting to penalize classification errors proportionally on minority classes:
\begin{equation}
W_c = \frac{N_{total}}{K \cdot N_c}
\end{equation}
where $K$ represents the total number of classes and $N_c$ is the frequency of class $c$.

\subsection{Paradigm 2: Word2Vec + Stacked Bidirectional LSTM}
To capture non-linear temporal dynamics and forward-backward semantic dependencies, we constructed a deep recurrent architecture (Figure~\ref{fig:bilstm_arch}).

The model projects sequences of token indices into 128-dimensional continuous vector embeddings. The BiLSTM layer processes input sequence $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T)$ through both forward and backward recurrence:
\begin{align}
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t &= \text{LSTM}_{fwd}(\mathbf{x}_t, \overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t-1}) \\
\overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t &= \text{LSTM}_{bwd}(\mathbf{x}_t, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t+1}) \\
\mathbf{h}_t &= [\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t \,\|\, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t]
\end{align}
The final representation combines the terminal forward hidden state and initial backward hidden state $[\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_T \,\|\, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_1] \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$, routed through spatial dropout ($p=0.3$), dense linear transformation, and categorical softmax cross-entropy loss.

\subsection{Paradigm 3: Pre-Trained Transformer (\texttt{BanglaBERT})}
To achieve deep contextual representations, we fine-tuned the pre-trained \texttt{BanglaBERT} architecture~\cite{alam2021banglabert}. \texttt{BanglaBERT} is built on the ELECTRA pre-training objective, trained over 27.5 gigabytes of diverse crawled Bengali corpora comprising newspapers, Wikipedia, blogs, and social platforms.

For sequence classification, the output representation of the classification token $\mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]}$ is passed into an MLP classification head:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{W}_o \tanh(\mathbf{W}_h \mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]} + \mathbf{b}_h) + \mathbf{b}_o)
\end{equation}
All 12 transformer encoder layers (comprising 110 million parameters) were unfrozen and trained end-to-end using the AdamW optimizer with a linear warmup learning rate schedule of $\eta = 2 \times 10^{-5}$ on a Google Colab T4 GPU platform.

\subsection{Dynamic Context-Aware Semantic Inversion}
Figure~\ref{fig:decision_flow} outlines the inference engine that resolves the Sarcastic Inversion paradox. When a sentence is evaluated, the Sarcasm and Sentiment models predict concurrently. If Sarcasm is asserted with high confidence ($P(\text{Sarcastic}) > 0.65$), any initial positive sentiment prediction is flipped to \textbf{Negative}, while Hate Speech classification flags potential cyberbullying or toxic harassment.

\subsection{Empirical Analysis by Task}
\textbf{1. Hate Speech Detection (Clear Transformer Dominance):} \\
Fine-tuned \texttt{BanglaBERT} emerged as the definitive champion on Hate Speech detection, achieving an outstanding \textbf{91.59\% accuracy} and \textbf{91.58\% Macro F1-score}. This represents a substantial +3.07\% improvement over the deep BiLSTM model (88.52\%) and +4.98\% over the statistical TF-IDF baseline (86.61\%). The superiority of \texttt{BanglaBERT} stems from self-attention heads that correctly disambiguate slurs from innocuous colloquial speech by analyzing bilateral syntactic context.

\textbf{2. Sarcasm Detection (Nuance Modeling):} \\
On Sarcasm detection, \texttt{BanglaBERT} established the state-of-the-art result at \textbf{76.76\% accuracy} and \textbf{74.89\% Macro F1}, outperforming both TF-IDF (74.94\%) and BiLSTM (74.36\%). Sarcasm heavily relies on subtle syntactic contradictions that require full self-attention across the sentence.

\textbf{3. Sentiment Analysis (Imbalance Dynamics):} \\
In Sentiment classification across the massive 156k dataset, raw test accuracy reached \textbf{76.39\%} (\texttt{BanglaBERT}) to \textbf{77.60\%} (TF-IDF), with Weighted F1-scores exceeding \textbf{81\%}. The lower Macro F1 ($\sim$51.5--53.7\%) is a direct statistical consequence of severe class imbalance in public Bengali sentiment datasets (where positive samples exceed 88\% of the corpus). This demonstrates that while the models possess high overall predictive fidelity, balanced macro-averaging prevents majority-class bias.

\section{Threats to Validity \& Future Work}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\textbf{1. Banglish and Code-Mixed Text:} Colloquial comments frequently intersperse Bengali with English loanwords or phonetic Romanized script. While our custom tokenization filters basic noise, joint multilingual pre-training remains an open challenge.

\textbf{2. Extreme Sentiment Skew:} Incorporating focal loss or synthetic minority oversampling (SMOTE) on sentence embeddings could further bolster Macro F1 on minority neutral and negative sentiment instances.

\textbf{3. Multi-Task Hard Parameter Sharing:} Future investigations will explore unified multi-task loss architectures where a single shared transformer trunk branches into task-specific heads, optimizing shared feature representations.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
In this project, we developed a comprehensive, context-aware multi-task natural language processing framework for Bengali social media text. By constructing a 218k+ sample corpus spanning Sentiment, Sarcasm, and Hate Speech, we systematically evaluated three progressive modeling paradigms: statistical ML (TF-IDF + LR), deep sequential networks (Word2Vec + Stacked BiLSTM), and modern transformers (\texttt{BanglaBERT}). Fine-tuned \texttt{BanglaBERT} demonstrated state-of-the-art efficacy, attaining \textbf{91.59\% accuracy and 91.58\% Macro F1} on Hate Speech, and \textbf{76.76\% accuracy and 74.89\% Macro F1} on Sarcasm. Combined with a negation-preserving preprocessing pipeline and an automated sarcastic inversion engine, this work presents a robust, end-to-end solution for contextual Bengali text classification.
